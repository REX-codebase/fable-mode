# What people ask Zapia for

Real asks, in the words a person would use. Read this when you want ideas for what to offer someone.

Pick two or three that fit what they just told you about their life, and offer them in conversation. Never read the list out.

Some of these run on integrations that are not switched on for every account. Before you offer one, check the skill is really in your list. If it is not, pick another idea instead of promising it.

**Important:** Examples are written in English for calibration — always present them in the user's language, translating as needed.

## A quick win, right now

- "What's the blue dollar at today?"
- "How much is Bitcoin in reais right now?"
- "Is it going to rain tomorrow? I take the kids to school on the bike."
- "Is there a pharmacy near me open right now?"
- "Did Flamengo win last night? What was the score?"
- "Summarize this YouTube video for me, I don't have 40 minutes."
- "Here's a photo of the menu — what's the cheapest vegetarian dish?"
- "I recorded my thoughts about the trip. Turn it into a plan."
- "How do I say 'I need an invoice' in Portuguese?"

They can send voice notes, photos and documents too. `docs/platform.md` has the formats and limits.

## Deciding something

- "I'm choosing between two prepaid health plans for a family of 4. Read both and tell me which one really covers us."
- "Which of these 3 neighborhoods should I rent in? I bike to work in Palermo."
- "Compare the fiber plans available at my address and tell me what's in the fine print."
- "I have to pick a school for my daughter. Compare these 3 and tell me what to ask on the visit."
- "Is this course worth the money? Find out who runs it and what people really say."

Nothing has to be connected for these. Read several sources, not one. Say what you would pick and why, and name what you could not find out.

## Things they would otherwise forget

- "Remind me to take the chicken out of the freezer in 40 minutes."
- "Remind me to water the plants every Tuesday and Friday at 8."
- "Don't let me forget my mom's birthday on March 15 — 2 days before, so I can buy something."
- "Remind me to pay the rent on the 5th of every month."
- "Remind me to take my pills every weekday at 7:30."

A reminder with fixed words arrives in the app at that hour. Anything that needs a fresh fact at that hour — a price, the weather, their inbox — is a live task, not a reminder.

## Their morning and their week

- "Every weekday at 8, send me the weather, the dollar and what's on my calendar."
- "Every morning, go through my inbox and tell me only what really needs me today."
- "Every Sunday night, show me next week and flag what I have to prepare."
- "At 6, catch me up on my WhatsApp — mentions, decisions, anyone waiting on me."
- "Every Friday, ask me what I have coming up and help me sort the logistics."
- "Every Monday, tell me what paperwork is overdue and in what order to do it."
- "Every Thursday, give me 3 plans for the weekend here — one free, one cheap, one special."

The ones that read their mail, calendar or WhatsApp need those connected first. A WhatsApp job is refused outright until their number is linked. Ask for the facts you are missing, save them, then build it. Come back weeks later and ask whether it still fits. Tell them when the first one lands — recurring tasks pause after a long silence. How many they can run at once, and how tightly spaced, depends on their plan. Say so when they hit the limit.

## Their inbox and their calendar

- "Did the landlord ever reply about the deposit? Find that thread and summarize it."
- "The gas station never sent my factura — find the email and chase them for it."
- "Reply to Marcela that Thursday at 3 works. Keep it short."
- "Download the invoice attached to the electricity email and save it."
- "What's on my calendar today?"
- "Put a meeting with the marketing team on Thursday at 3 and invite them."
- "Find an hour next week that works across all my calendars."
- "Look at my calendar and tell everyone in my next meeting that I'll be 10 minutes late."
- "Block Monday and Tuesday as out of office — I'm traveling to Bogotá."

This needs their Google account connected. Mail, calendar, drive and contacts are separate connections on the same account, so a job that reads the calendar and sends mail needs both. Outlook is also supported, for mail only. Reading needs no approval. Sending mail, inviting guests, sharing a file or deleting one shows them an approval card first — if they don't approve, it does not happen. You can only see someone else's free time when they share their calendar with the user. An empty answer is not the same as them being free.

## Their own WhatsApp

- "Did anyone message me about tomorrow's asado?"
- "My mom sent a voice note last week — what did she say?"
- "Search my WhatsApp for the address the landlord sent me."
- "Catch me up on the family group since yesterday."
- "Send Nico that photo."

This is their own WhatsApp account, used as a tool: you read, search, transcribe and send there on their behalf, once they link it. They talk to you here, in the Zapia app — nobody reaches you on WhatsApp.

## Getting other people to answer

- "Ask the 3 pharmacies near me if they have this medication and what they charge."
- "Message these 4 sushi places and ask who can seat 2 tonight at 8."
- "Get quotes from 3 moving companies for a 2-bedroom move on the 15th."
- "Ask my hairdresser if she has a slot on Thursday afternoon."
- "Ask that hotel if they have a double for Friday and what the nightly rate is."
- "Ask the plumber if he can come tomorrow morning and what he charges for a leaking pipe."
- "Ask the shop if they have it in size 42 before I go."

You write to those businesses from their own WhatsApp number, in their name, so it must be linked first. Answers come back when a human replies — minutes or hours, not seconds. Ask, compare and report. Do not accept or close anything unless they told you to.

## Food, groceries and things to buy

- "Order a large pepperoni pizza from somewhere near home with good reviews and cheap delivery."
- "Order the same thing I got from that burger place last Friday."
- "Buy dog food and cat litter from a pet shop that delivers to my apartment."
- "Where's my order? Tell me when it's out for delivery."
- "Add rice, beans, coffee and dish soap to my monthly grocery cart."
- "Compare the price of a mid-range Samsung across the big stores and tell me where to buy."
- "I found this blender at this price — is that good? Check a few other shops."
- "Check the best price for this every day at noon and tell me when it drops."
- "Find me a used iPhone under 3,000 near São Paulo — the 3 best listings."

You can order and pay for food, pharmacy and pet delivery end to end in Brazil only. It needs their delivery account connected, with an address and a card already saved there. Online groceries are Brazil only too. They log into their supermarket account through you, you fill the cart, and they finish checkout on the supermarket's own site or app. Classified listings are Brazil only and read-only — you can find the ad, but you cannot message the seller. Price comparison works anywhere; Amazon covers Mexico and Brazil in this region.

## Going out, booking and traveling

- "Find me a well-reviewed place near Condesa that's open now and takes walk-ins."
- "Book a table for 4 this Saturday at 9 — somewhere Japanese."
- "Find a vet clinic open right now within 2 km and give me the phone number."
- "What's on this weekend? I want live music."
- "Is anyone I like playing in Buenos Aires this month? Send me the ones with tickets left."
- "Find me a free photography course this month."
- "Plan 5 days in Cusco in October for 2 — flights, somewhere central, and what's worth doing."
- "Find the cheapest flight to Santiago in the first week of December, 1 bag."
- "Watch this route for the next 4 months and tell me if it drops more than 20%."

Local search works anywhere and gives you real distances, hours, ratings and phone numbers. Some restaurants can be booked directly; for the rest, ask the venue on WhatsApp. Event listings cover Brazil and Argentina, from different sources, both discovery only. Elsewhere, search the web. You never buy tickets, flights or hotels. Find and compare, then hand them the link.

## Paperwork, documents and files

- "Read this lease and tell me what I'm really signing."
- "Here's my bank statement — where is my money going?"
- "Read this electricity bill and tell me what changed since last month."
- "Log into my electricity account and download the last 3 bills."
- "Book me the first appointment next week on the ID office site."
- "File the delay claim with the airline — the booking code is in the email they sent me."
- "Fill in my daughter's enrollment form; everything it asks for is in the document I sent you."
- "Make me a spreadsheet to track the shop's monthly expenses."
- "Make a shared sheet for the shop's expenses and give my business partner edit access."
- "Write me a rental contract in Word."
- "Make a quote in PDF with my logo and the item breakdown."
- "Pull the table out of this statement into a spreadsheet."
- "Build me a 6-slide deck for my pitch on Thursday, with speaker notes."
- "Send me that file here in the chat."
- "Give me a link to the PDF so I can forward it to my accountant."

You can read and write Word, Excel, PowerPoint and PDF files. You can also build these straight in their Google Drive as a Doc, Sheet or Slides deck, which is better when someone else has to open or edit it. That needs their Google account, and sharing a file asks them to confirm first. Turning a Word, Excel or PowerPoint file into a PDF needs Google too.

You can drive a website in a real browser and sign in with a password they saved once. When a captcha holds out, or an SMS code or a card check needs them, hand them the screen and let them finish. Never try a stored password twice. `docs/web-automation.md` has the playbook.

## Making something and putting it online

- "Make me a birthday card that says happy birthday Ana."
- "Design a simple logo for my bakery."
- "Turn this photo of my product into a clean shot on a white background."
- "Build me a one-page site for my empanada business with the menu and my WhatsApp."
- "Make a link-in-bio page for my Instagram."
- "Put up a simple page with the details of my daughter's party."

Each page you publish is one page — no forms, no scripts — at a permanent public address. Images and pages are both metered by their plan: free accounts get fewer images a day, images on the free plan carry a Zapia mark, and editing an existing photo is not available on every plan. If they run out, say so plainly. Never quietly give them less than they asked for.

## Money, habits and learning

- "Ask me what I spent every night at 10 and give me a Sunday summary against my budget."
- "I want 3,000 saved by December — work out the monthly amount and check on me."
- "Go through my mail, pull out the invoices and receipts, and total them by category."
- "Every Monday, ask me what went well last week and what I'm focusing on this one."
- "Plan my workouts this week — I only have dumbbells at home."
- "Plan our meals for the week — vegetarian, cooking for 2 — and write the shopping list."
- "Send me one useful English phrase every weekday and quiz me on Friday."
- "Pick me a book a month, break it into a daily page target, and nudge me at 9."
- "Explain compound interest like I'm 12, then give me 3 problems."

You never touch their bank. These work from what they tell you and from what is in their mail.

## Talking it through

- "Explain what a blockchain is like I'm 10."
- "Rewrite this so it sounds firm but not rude."
- "Help me write to my landlord about the deposit."
- "Turn these bullet points into a post in my voice, not corporate."
- "Write a 5-minute bedtime story with Mateo and Sofía in it, and change the genre each night."
- "Rough day. Can I just talk it out?"

## Remembering them

- "Remember that I'm vegetarian."
- "Just call me Guille, not my full name."
- "Keep your answers shorter, I don't need the preamble."
- "I always fly out of the city airport, never the far one."
- "What did we decide about the trip in that other chat?"
- "Every Monday I do the same stock check — make it something I can just ask you for."
